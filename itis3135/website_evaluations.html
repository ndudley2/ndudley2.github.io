Website Evaluations

The University of North Carolina at Charlotte

The website for our school. I think it looks pretty nice!

  • Contrast: White text is used against dark colors and black text is used against light colors. Everything is legible. Background color and font size are used to indicate different sections. It's clear where the header and footer are. Links are indicated through a button shape or underlined text when you hover over them, which allows them to stand out among regular text.
  • Repetition: Aside from the images, the website has a consistent color scheme utilizing the school colors. The same few fonts are used consistently and they don't clash with each other.
  • Alignment: Text is usually left-aligned within its box. Sections containing only links or images are centered for emphasis. If an image has a diagonal border, the text next to it is aligned on that angle, too.
  • Proximity: The body has a width slightly smaller than the viewport, pushing everything closer to the center. Text is clearly placed next to the related link or image, if applicable. Within the footer, links are sorted into groups indicating that they are related to the group's label.

Astrud Gilberto - Official Homepage

The official website for the late Astrud Gilberto, a Brazilian bossa nova singer. She had a beautiful voice. You may have heard her if you've listened to the original version of "The Girl from Ipanema". Her website... not so beautiful.

  • Contrast: The colors contrast pretty well, and font and text size are used to indicate sections and hierarchy. However, quotes are written in Lucida Handwriting, which is not a good choice for long pieces of text, especially in a small size. These parts are hard to read.
  • Repetition: Links are consistently blue and text is consistently purple with ONE exception: one of the paragraphs starts with a black letter "N". There are a lot of different fonts and they are seemingly not used with much rhyme or reason. Text is underlined or bolded or italicized for emphasis, but it's not consistent either. For example, if a section of text ends with a dash, sometimes the dash is underlined, sometimes it isn't. Sometimes, a line of asterisks is used to separate two paragraphs, but the placement is seemingly random. For example, a line of asterisks can sometimes be between two related paragraphs, and sometimes, a completely different topic will start WITHOUT a line of asterisks.
  • Alignment: This is my biggest problem with this page. Some paragraphs are centered, some are right-aligned, and there's seemingly no intention behind it. Worst of all, the sidebar, which is only visible at the top of the page, pushes EVERYTHING ELSE ON THE PAGE TO THE RIGHT. So once you scroll down, it looks like the page is off-center for no reason.
  • Proximity: Similar to the UNC Charlotte website, the body has a smaller width than the viewport, pushing things closer together, which I like. However, the order of this page makes no sense. It starts with some important information about Gilberto's online presence, then an advertisement for the store, then a section on animal rights activism, then an interview, then another section on animal rights activism, then ANOTHER notice about her online presence, ANOTHER interview, ANOTHER image related to caring for animals, and for some reason, a seemingly unrelated Confucius quote at the end. Ideally, related information should all be in the same section. Also, as I mentioned earlier, sometimes related paragraphs are separated with a line of asterisks.
